Otter vs Fathom

Otter is strongest as a live transcript and searchable meeting workspace. Fathom gives individuals a more generous recording archive and stronger video follow-up.

10 min read · Updated July 2026

The short answer

Pick Otter when seeing and editing a live transcript matters, or when you want a familiar shared workspace for notes, chat, and action items across meetings.

Pick Fathom when unlimited recording and transcription on the free plan, video playback, clips, and polished post-call summaries matter more than following every word live.

Both products are meeting assistants. If your goal is to improve how you communicate rather than only document calls, Work Coach analyzes your delivery and gives private practice based on your real meetings.

What Otter does

Otter centers the live transcript. Its assistant can join Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams, label speakers, capture slides, generate a summary, and answer questions about the conversation. People can follow along, highlight text, add comments, and assign action items while the meeting is still running.

Otter also offers a desktop app for bot-free capture. That newer path is useful when a visible participant is unwelcome, while OtterPilot remains the more automated option for calendar meetings.

What Fathom does

Fathom records meetings, produces transcripts and summaries, and makes the video easy to navigate and share. Its free individual plan includes unlimited recordings, storage, and transcription. Paid plans unlock advanced summary templates and more workflow features.

Fathom historically joined as a visible bot. Its newer bot-free experience keeps a consent signal through meeting chat or an on-screen prompt, and the rollout still varies by meeting platform and operating system. That makes capture details worth checking for your exact setup.

Capture and platform support

OtterPilot is a participant in the meeting and can be scheduled from your calendar. That makes automation simple, but guests see the assistant. Otter's desktop recorder supplies a bot-free option and captures audio from the computer.

Fathom also began with a visible participant. Its current bot-free rollout can capture locally, but Fathom still discloses recording through chat or a prompt. In July 2026, support differs across Zoom, Meet, Teams, Mac, and Windows, so do not assume every combination behaves the same.

Notes, search, and workflows

Otter is better when the transcript itself is the working surface. Participants can read along, search, edit, comment, and use Otter AI Chat during or after the call. It feels closer to a collaborative meeting notebook.

Fathom is better when the recording is the durable artifact. A viewer can jump from a summary or clip to the exact moment in the video. Its team products add shared calls, playlists, search, and higher-tier coaching scorecards.

Free plans and pricing

Otter Basic includes 300 minutes each month with a 30-minute meeting limit. Pro is $8.33 per user per month billed annually or $16.99 monthly and includes 1,200 minutes. Business is $19.99 annually or $30 monthly and advertises unlimited meeting minutes. See Otter pricing.

Fathom Free includes unlimited recordings, storage, and transcription, but advanced AI summaries are limited after the first five calls each month. Premium is $15 per user per month. Team and Team Pro are $19 and $29 per user per month when billed annually. See Fathom pricing.

Privacy and AI training

Otter says it uses deidentified user data to train and improve its proprietary AI unless the user opts out. Enterprise buyers should review admin controls, consent, retention, and model settings rather than relying only on the meeting bot's presence.

Fathom says its third-party AI providers cannot train on customer data. Fathom may use deidentified data to improve its own models, with an opt-out for users and organizations. Its security documentation lists SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and HIPAA controls.

A different option: coaching for the person in the meeting

Both products in this comparison are built to preserve and distribute what happened. That still leaves a personal question unanswered: how well did you handle the conversation?

Work Coach records locally on Mac or Windows with no bot, then coaches only the account owner. It applies role-aware rubrics, links feedback to timestamped evidence, counts filler words with playback, reviews voice delivery, and turns real moments into voice practice. The results never appear in a manager dashboard or shared meeting report. It costs $50 a month after a two-week free trial.

Frequently asked questions

Is Fathom really free for unlimited meetings?

Yes. Fathom's individual Free plan includes unlimited recordings, storage, and transcription. Advanced summary formats become limited after the first five calls in a month.

Does Otter or Fathom provide a live transcript?

Otter is the clearer choice for a transcript you can follow and edit during the meeting. Fathom is more focused on recording and post-meeting review.

Can Otter and Fathom record without a bot?

Both now have bot-free capture paths, but platform and operating-system support varies. Fathom still provides a consent signal rather than recording silently.

Which is better for sales coaching?

Fathom's higher team plan offers scorecards and team coaching. Otter has sales workflows and conversation insights. Work Coach is more suitable when the coaching should remain private to the individual.

Which is better for privacy?

Neither has one universal answer. Fathom says AI vendors cannot train on customer data; Otter publishes a deidentified proprietary training practice with an opt-out. Your capture mode, workspace settings, and company contract matter.
