Read.ai vs Otter

Otter gives you a strong live transcript and lower entry price. Read.ai measures more about meeting quality, engagement, and speaker behavior.

10 min read · Updated July 2026

The short answer

Pick Read.ai when you want automated meeting scores, engagement and sentiment signals, speaker coaching metrics, 20+ languages, and reports that extend into email and messaging activity.

Pick Otter when you want a lower-cost searchable transcript workspace, a strong live transcript, collaborative editing, and straightforward notes for English and its smaller language set.

Pick Work Coach if feedback should focus deeply on your communication and remain private rather than appearing in a shared meeting score or manager-facing workspace.

What Read.ai does

Read.ai records meetings, creates transcripts and summaries, and calculates a Read Score from factors such as engagement and sentiment. Speaker Coach reports talk time, talk-to-listen ratio, filler words, interruptions, pace, and charisma. These metrics make Read more analytical than a standard note taker.

The assistant normally joins as a visible participant. Read also supports local capture for some unsupported meeting environments. Paid plans add search, playback, integrations, and larger cross-meeting workspaces.

What Otter does

Otter emphasizes the transcript as a live shared surface. OtterPilot joins scheduled Zoom, Meet, and Teams calls, captures slides, labels speakers, creates a summary and action items, and supports questions through Otter AI Chat.

A desktop app supplies bot-free capture. Otter has fewer transcription languages than Read, but its entry-level paid plan costs less and its collaborative transcript experience is mature.

Capture and platform support

Read's assistant can automatically join calendar meetings and makes recording clear to attendees. Its local capture broadens support beyond the standard video platforms. Read also connects meeting intelligence with email and messaging summaries inside its workspace.

OtterPilot follows the same automated-participant model for common video platforms. Otter's desktop app gives users a bot-free path when they prefer to capture system audio locally. In both products, the person recording remains responsible for appropriate consent.

Notes, search, and workflows

Read produces a denser diagnostic report. A manager or participant can see the transcript, summary, reactions, engagement patterns, sentiment, speaker distribution, and behavioral metrics. The score is useful for scanning many meetings, but users should treat inferred sentiment and charisma as signals rather than objective truth.

Otter is more document-like. People can read along, edit the transcript, highlight, comment, assign action items, and ask questions. It is a better fit when the team wants a shared source of written meeting memory without a prominent performance score.

Free plans and pricing

Read Free includes five meeting reports per month, typically capped at one hour. Pro is $19.75 monthly or $15 per user per month billed annually. Enterprise is $29.75 monthly or $22.50 annually; Enterprise+ is $39.75 or $29.75. See Read pricing.

Otter Basic includes 300 minutes per month and a 30-minute meeting cap. Pro is $8.33 per user per month billed annually or $16.99 monthly for 1,200 minutes. Business is $19.99 annually or $30 monthly. See Otter pricing.

Privacy and AI training

Read says it does not use customer data to train generative AI models by default and lists SOC 2 controls. Because performance signals can be sensitive, teams should decide who can see speaker and engagement analytics, how long reports remain, and whether employees understand their use.

Otter says it uses deidentified user data to train and improve proprietary AI unless the user opts out. Admins should review that choice along with workspace access and retention. The same transcript can feel like a personal aid or a monitoring artifact depending on who controls it.

A different option: coaching for the person in the meeting

Both products in this comparison are built to preserve and distribute what happened. That still leaves a personal question unanswered: how well did you handle the conversation?

Work Coach records locally on Mac or Windows with no bot, then coaches only the account owner. It applies role-aware rubrics, links feedback to timestamped evidence, counts filler words with playback, reviews voice delivery, and turns real moments into voice practice. The results never appear in a manager dashboard or shared meeting report. It costs $50 a month after a two-week free trial.

Frequently asked questions

Is Read.ai or Otter cheaper?

Otter Pro has the lower annual entry price at $8.33 per user per month. Read Pro is $15 annually and includes a broader analytics layer.

Which is better for live transcription?

Otter is the clearer choice if reading, editing, and collaborating on the transcript during the meeting is the priority.

Which has better speaker analytics?

Read.ai measures more dimensions, including talk time, filler words, interruptions, pace, engagement, sentiment, and charisma. Work Coach goes deeper on private coaching and practice rather than team scoring.

Do Read.ai and Otter join as bots?

Their automated assistants normally join as visible participants. Both also provide local or desktop capture options for some bot-free workflows.

Which supports more languages?

Read.ai publishes support for more than 20 languages. Otter currently supports six named transcription languages.
