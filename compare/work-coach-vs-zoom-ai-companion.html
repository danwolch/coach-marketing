Work Coach vs Zoom AI Companion

AI Companion comes free with paid Zoom plans and takes solid notes. It also answers to Zoom, your admin, and whoever hosts the call. Work Coach answers to you. Here is how they compare.

10 min read · Updated July 2026

The short answer

Use Zoom AI Companion if your work happens in Zoom and it is turned on for you. It is a capable note-taker bundled at no extra cost with paid Zoom plans: summaries with action items, in-meeting questions, missed-meeting catch-up, and no bot in the participant list. Free is hard to argue with.

Use Work Coach if you want feedback on how you communicated, which AI Companion does not offer at all, or if you need something that works regardless of which app the meeting is on, which plan the host has, or what your admin decided. Work Coach records locally on your computer, coaches only you, and reports only to you.

The two barely overlap. One is a convenience feature of a meeting platform; the other is a personal coach that happens to watch your meetings.

What Zoom AI Companion does

AI Companion is Zoom's native AI assistant, woven through Zoom Workplace and bundled at no extra cost with every paid plan. That bundling is the core pitch, and it is a good one: if your company already pays for Zoom, the note-taker is already there.

In meetings, it generates summaries with action items, drawing on the audio, screen-share text, and chat. Participants can ask it questions mid-meeting ("what did I miss?"), and it drafts messages in Team Chat, email, and Whiteboard. The 3.0 release, generally available since November 2025, added agentic tasks and cross-app search. Transcription covers 36 languages in meetings, and summaries are attributed by speaker. Since this is a platform feature rather than a bot, nothing extra appears in the participant list for Zoom meetings; attendees see a pop-up notice and a persistent sparkle icon when it is active.

The catches sit in the plumbing. AI Companion works only in Zoom, unless you pay $12 per user per month for the Custom AI Companion add-on, which covers external Google Meet and Teams meetings by sending in a visible bot. It runs only when the host enables it, and only if the host is on a paid plan. Admins can switch it off account-wide. And summaries auto-delete after 30 days by default, which surprises people.

What Work Coach does

Work Coach is a desktop app for Mac (macOS 15+) and Windows 11 that treats your meetings as raw material for coaching. It captures audio locally, with no bot and no platform dependency, then analyzes how you communicated.

Alongside the standard transcript and editable summary, you get:

  • Coaching scores tuned to the meeting. Work Coach identifies the meeting type (12 of them, from interviews to 1:1s to pipeline reviews) and your role, then scores what matters for that role, with timestamped evidence you can play back. A manager gets scored on psychological safety in a 1:1; an interviewee on the shape of their answers.
  • Filler-word analysis. A deterministic counter of your ums and likes per minute, with a rating band and audio playback of every instance, in English, Spanish, French, and German.
  • Voice delivery scores. Up to five high-stakes moments of your speech analyzed as audio, for pitch, pace, pauses, projection, and tone.
  • Practice roleplay. Live voice rehearsal against an AI playing the other person, built from your real meetings, with scored reps.
  • A coach chat with memory. Ask whether your promotion case is landing or whether you rambled in the standup, and get an answer grounded in your actual conversations.

No admin provisions it, no host enables it, and no employer can see into it. You install it, you press record, and the feedback is yours.

How they record your meetings

In a Zoom meeting, AI Companion is about as unobtrusive as meeting AI gets. The host turns it on, everyone sees the notice and the sparkle icon, and there is no bot squatting in the participant list. Admins can even require a consent prompt, where declining keeps you in the meeting with mic and camera off.

Notice, though, how many people other than you appear in that sentence. The host decides whether it runs. The admin decides whether the host is allowed to run it. If the host is an outside recruiter on a free Basic plan, or your company disabled the feature, you get nothing, and there is no button you can press to change that. Off Zoom, the story ends entirely unless someone pays for the add-on, which works by sending a visible bot into Meet and Teams calls.

Work Coach removes every one of those dependencies. It runs in your menu bar, notices when any meeting app starts using your microphone, and asks whether you want to record. Audio is captured directly on your computer, so Zoom, Meet, Teams, Slack huddles, Webex, and FaceTime all work identically, whoever hosts and whatever plan they have. It never records without you pressing start. Because nothing announces itself to the room, disclosing that you record is your call and your responsibility, as with any local recorder.

What you get after the meeting

AI Companion's summary is a fine baseline: main topics, action items, speaker attribution, delivered to the host and shared per policy. Reviewers find it accurate on clear audio and weaker in crosstalk, and the recap can miss nuance. Then, 30 days later, it deletes itself unless someone changed the default. As a lightweight record of what happened, it does the job well enough that plenty of teams never need a third-party note-taker.

What it will never tell you is how you did. No filler-word count, no talk metrics, no feedback of any kind on your communication. Zoom simply does not do coaching in AI Companion, on any plan or add-on.

Work Coach starts where the summary stops. It tells you that you hedged for twelve seconds before your recommendation, plays you the clip, scores how you ran your 1:1 with your manager, and offers a practice rep so the next version comes out clean. The transcript is the raw material; the coaching is the product.

Where Zoom keeps its coaching

Zoom does sell communication analytics, in a product called Zoom Revenue Accelerator. It tracks talk-listen ratio, filler words, talking speed, patience, longest spiel, and sentiment, and in July 2026 Zoom relaunched it with real-time guidance, AI roleplay, and new tiers at $66 and $99.99 per user per month.

Look at who it is for, though. ZRA is a sales tool bought by sales organizations, and its metrics are visible to reps and their managers by design. It exists so a manager can review the team's calls. That is a legitimate product, and if you run a sales team it may be exactly what you want.

Work Coach inverts the audience. The same general category of feedback, plus voice delivery analysis and role-aware scoring across all twelve meeting types rather than sales calls alone, and every word of it goes to you and no one else. Your manager cannot buy access to it. If you are rehearsing a promotion ask, that difference is the whole point.

Privacy and who sees your data

Zoom deserves credit on AI training. After a 2023 terms-of-service backlash, it adopted an explicit commitment not to use customer audio, video, chat, or screen-share content to train its own or third-party AI models, and that commitment has held. Admin controls are granular, data residency options exist, and summaries default to host-only visibility.

The structural fact remains that on a company account, the company owns the machinery. Admins set whether AI Companion runs, where summaries go, and how long anything is retained. Meeting summaries on your employer's Zoom account are your employer's records. None of that is sinister; it is what a workplace platform is. It just makes Zoom's AI a strange place to process the meetings that are about your own career.

Work Coach is built for exactly those meetings. Audio stays on your computer and auto-deletes after 30 days; no audio files are stored in the cloud. Transcripts and coaching feedback live in your private account, never train shared models, and are never visible to your employer, your manager, or HR, a commitment written into the privacy policy. If you change jobs, your coaching history goes with you.

Pricing

AI Companion itself costs nothing beyond a paid Zoom plan; Zoom Pro runs roughly $13 to $16 per user per month as of mid-2026, and free Basic accounts get no AI features. The Custom AI Companion add-on for cross-platform meetings and custom agents is $12 per user per month, and Zoom Revenue Accelerator's new tiers were announced at $66 and $99.99. Current details are on Zoom's pricing page.

Work Coach is $50 a month after a two-week free trial, no credit card required. Comparing it to a free bundled feature misses what the $50 buys: it is priced as coaching, against the benchmark of an executive coach at $5,000 a month, not as a note-taker. Tiers with human coaches cost $250 (async review of your progress) and $500 (a monthly live session).

If notes are all you need and your host has a paid plan, AI Companion wins on price forever. The comparison only becomes interesting when what you want is to get better.

Can you use both together?

Yes, and it is the natural setup for anyone whose company runs on Zoom. Let AI Companion keep writing the team's summaries; Work Coach records the same meeting locally and produces the private debrief. Neither interferes with the other.

Work Coach also covers everything AI Companion cannot reach: the interview on the recruiter's Meet link, the Teams call with a client, the Slack huddle. And it can import transcripts from other tools, so meetings that only exist as someone else's summary can still get a coaching pass.

Which one should you pick?

Use Zoom AI Companion if you...

  • Live in Zoom and your admin has it enabled
  • Want free summaries with your existing paid plan
  • Like asking "what did I miss?" mid-meeting
  • Need 36-language transcription on Zoom calls
  • Only need a short-term record, not an archive

Pick Work Coach if you...

  • Want feedback on how you communicate, which AI Companion never provides
  • Meet on more than one platform, or with hosts on free plans
  • Refuse to let an admin or host decide what tools you get
  • Want to practice hard conversations before they happen
  • Need certainty that your employer never sees your feedback

Frequently asked questions

Is Zoom AI Companion free?

It is included at no extra cost with paid Zoom Workplace plans, starting with Pro at roughly $13 to $16 per user per month. It is not available on Zoom's free Basic plan, so the meeting host needs a paid plan for anyone to get a summary. The Custom AI Companion add-on, which covers meetings on Google Meet and Teams, costs an extra $12 per user per month.

Does Zoom AI Companion do coaching?

No. Base AI Companion has no speaker metrics or communication feedback of any kind. Zoom's coaching features, including talk-listen ratio, filler words, and talking speed, live in Zoom Revenue Accelerator, a separate sales product where the metrics are visible to reps and their managers, with paid tiers announced in July 2026 at $66 and $99.99 per user per month. Work Coach gives you role-aware coaching on every kind of meeting, and the feedback goes only to you.

Can my employer control Zoom AI Companion?

Yes, fully. On business accounts AI Companion is gated at the account, group, and user level, so if your admin disables it you cannot turn it on. The host also controls whether it runs in any given meeting. Work Coach has no admin controls at all: it is a personal app on your computer that no employer can switch off, and no employer can see into.

Does Zoom AI Companion work with Google Meet or Teams?

Only with the Custom AI Companion add-on at $12 per user per month, which sends a visible bot into external Google Meet and Teams meetings. Base AI Companion works in Zoom meetings only. Work Coach captures audio locally on your computer, so it works the same way in Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Slack huddles, Webex, and anything else your machine plays.

Do Zoom AI Companion summaries expire?

By default, yes. Meeting summaries auto-delete after 30 days, which regularly surprises people who go looking for an old decision. Work Coach keeps your transcripts, summaries, and coaching history in your private account for as long as you keep the account, and they stay with you if you change jobs.
