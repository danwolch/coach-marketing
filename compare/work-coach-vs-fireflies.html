Work Coach vs Fireflies.ai

Fireflies turns your team's calls into a searchable workspace of notes, clips, and CRM updates. Work Coach watches the same meetings locally and coaches you, privately. Here is where each one actually wins.

11 min read · Updated July 2026

The short answer

Pick Fireflies.ai if your team needs one place where every call becomes a transcript, a summary, and an update in the tools you already use. It transcribes in 100+ languages, pushes notes to Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot, and Notion, and its channels turn meetings into a shared library the whole company can search.

Pick Work Coach if you care less about what the team decided and more about how you came across. It records locally with no bot, then coaches you: role-aware scores on how you handled the conversation, filler words per minute with playback, voice delivery analysis, and a practice mode. Fireflies does have real conversation analytics, but they sit on the Business plan ($19 per seat and up) and belong to the workspace, where managers use them to review reps.

Plenty of people run both. They listen to the same meeting and produce completely different things.

What Fireflies.ai does

Fireflies is one of the largest meeting notetakers, claiming 20 million users across more than 500,000 organizations as of 2026, profitable since 2023 and valued at $1 billion in a 2025 tender offer. The core loop: connect your calendar, and Fred, the Fireflies bot, joins your Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, or Webex calls as a participant labeled "Fireflies.ai Notetaker." It transcribes with speaker labels in 100+ languages, then delivers a summary with action items.

Around that loop sits a genuinely deep team product. AskFred answers questions across your meeting history. More than 200 AI Apps extract whatever a workflow needs, from objections to candidate answers. Soundbites turn moments into shareable clips, Topic Trackers follow themes across calls, and integrations push everything into Salesforce, HubSpot, Slack, Notion, and Asana, with a public API for the rest. Capture goes beyond video calls too: dialer integrations, mobile apps for in-person recording, and a Chrome extension that transcribes Google Meet without sending in the bot.

The center of gravity is the workspace. Meetings land in channels, recaps go out to attendees, and on Business and Enterprise plans admins get Conversation Intelligence dashboards across the team. If your company wants one searchable archive of every call, wired into the CRM, this is exactly what Fireflies was built for.

What Work Coach does

Work Coach asks a different question about the same meeting: what is holding you back? It is a desktop app for Mac and Windows that captures audio locally, with no bot, then analyzes how you communicated.

You still get the table stakes, a timestamped transcript and an editable summary with action items. The product is what sits on top:

  • Role-aware coaching scores. Each meeting gets classified (interview, 1:1, standup, sales call, presentation, among 12 types) along with your role in it, then scored 0-100 on the dimensions that matter for that role: STAR answer quality if you were the interviewee, psychological safety if you ran the 1:1, discovery depth if you were selling. Every score carries timestamped evidence.
  • Hear the moment. Any piece of feedback can play the actual audio behind it, so you hear what the coach heard.
  • Honest filler counts. A deterministic counter (not an AI guess) tracks your ums, likes, and you-knows per minute, tells you whether you are in a low or excessive band, and queues up every instance for playback.
  • Voice delivery. Up to five high-stakes moments of your own speech get analyzed as audio: pitch, pace, pauses, projection, and tone, scored out of 100 with specific suggestions.
  • Practice mode. Live voice roleplay against an AI playing the other side, with scenarios generated from your real meetings and scored reps that trend over time.
  • A memory. Cross-meeting patterns, private notes on the people you work with, and a coach chat that answers questions like "did I bury the recommendation?" with the exact clip queued up.

There is no workspace to share into. No channels, no CRM fields, no admin seat. Everything Work Coach produces is for you alone.

How they record your meetings

Fireflies runs on calendar sync. Once connected, Fred auto-joins the meetings you tell it to, sits in the participant list as Fireflies.ai Notetaker, and records from inside the call. The bot does not verbally announce itself, so whether attendees get a consent notice depends on host settings.

Visibility cuts both ways. Everyone can see that recording is happening, which is honest. Yet the most persistent Fireflies complaints in Reddit threads are about the bot joining meetings nobody invited it to, including, in one widely shared case, 12-step recovery meetings, and IT admins describe struggling to keep it out of their orgs. Recap emails that auto-send to every attendee are the other default that trips people up. There are botless paths, to be fair: the Chrome extension captures Google Meet in real time, and the mobile and desktop apps record in-person conversations. For Zoom, Teams, and Webex meetings, though, the bot is the mechanism.

Work Coach never joins a call on any platform. It sits in your menu bar, notices when a meeting app starts using the microphone, and asks whether you want to record. Nothing starts until you press the button, and nothing is ever visible to other participants. Because capture is local system audio, it works with Zoom, Meet, Teams, Slack huddles, Webex, FaceTime, and anything else your computer plays. The flip side is the same as with any local recorder: telling people you record is your call and your responsibility.

What you get after the meeting

From Fireflies: a transcript, a summary shaped by the template you chose, action items, clips, and answers from AskFred. For a team this is the point. Every call becomes an asset someone else can search, quote, or sync to a CRM record.

Fireflies also has real conversation analytics, and here the plan structure matters. Conversation Intelligence is gated to Business ($19 per seat and up) and Enterprise; Free and Pro users get none of it. When you do pay, the metrics are legitimately detailed: talk-to-listen ratio, filler-word counts, longest monologue, questions asked, silence time, words per minute, and sentiment. Notice where they live, though: in team dashboards built so admins can track each teammate's performance and coach them. Fireflies' own framing is managers reviewing reps. The analytics describe you, and they belong to the workspace.

Work Coach inverts that. The coaching is the product, it runs on every meeting, and it reports to an audience of one. Fireflies can tell a dashboard how many filler words you used; Work Coach plays each one back to you, with the count per minute and the band you landed in, so the number starts dropping. The same goes for the bigger stuff: instead of a ratio in someone else's report, you get a scored breakdown of how you handled the actual conversation, with audio behind every observation. If that is the loop you are after, the cut filler words playbook shows how people run it, and there is a similar one for speaking up more in meetings.

Privacy and who sees your data

Credit where due: Fireflies' security posture is solid. SOC 2 Type II, GDPR compliance, a HIPAA BAA on Enterprise, AES-256 encryption, and a stated policy of not training AI on customer data by default, with zero-day retention agreements with its model vendors. On paper that is stronger than several rivals.

The exposure is organizational rather than technical. A Fireflies workspace belongs to the company: meetings can auto-share into channels, recaps can auto-email every attendee, Business and Enterprise admins see per-teammate analytics, and the Super Admin role has full visibility into the workspace. None of that is a flaw when the goal is team knowledge. It changes the calculus when the recording is a 1:1 about your career, a job interview, or anything else you would not cc your manager on.

Work Coach is built so that question cannot come up. There is no workspace, no channels, no admin role, and no seat concept. Meeting audio stays on your computer and is deleted automatically after 30 days; no audio files are stored in the cloud. Transcripts and coaching live in your private account, and the privacy policy commits in writing that your employer, your manager, and HR cannot see them, that your data never trains shared models, and that your coaching history stays with you if you change jobs.

Pricing

Fireflies' free plan advertises unlimited transcription, and the transcription really is unlimited. The catch is everything around it: AI summary credits are limited (3 to 10 depending on how you sign up), AskFred and AI Apps draw from 20 monthly AI credits, and storage limits are listed inconsistently between the pricing page and the help docs. Trustpilot reviews regularly describe the free plan as feeling misleading once the summary credits run out.

Paid plans are billed per seat. Pro at $10 a month annual ($18 monthly) adds unlimited summary credits, video recording, and 8,000 minutes of storage per seat. Business at $19 annual ($29 monthly) adds unlimited storage and Conversation Intelligence. Enterprise at $39, annual only, adds HIPAA, private storage, and SSO. Current details are on the Fireflies pricing page.

Work Coach costs $50 a month after a two-week free trial with no credit card. That is five times Fireflies Pro, and it is the wrong comparison: Work Coach is priced as coaching, not note-taking. The benchmark it competes against is an executive coach at $5,000 a month, and the higher tiers add actual humans, with async review of your progress at $250 a month and a live monthly session at $500.

Can you use both together?

Yes, and nothing about the setup conflicts. Fred joins the call while Work Coach records locally; neither knows the other exists. Fireflies keeps doing the company's work, the CRM sync, the channels, and the shared notes, while Work Coach gives you the private debrief.

Work Coach also imports transcripts from other tools, so a meeting that only exists in Fireflies can still get a coaching pass after the fact.

Frequently asked questions

Does Fireflies work without a bot?

Partly. The Chrome extension can capture Google Meet in real time without the bot, and the mobile and desktop apps record in-person conversations. For Zoom, Microsoft Teams, and Webex meetings, Fred still joins as a visible participant. Work Coach never uses a bot anywhere: it captures audio locally on your Mac or Windows machine, and nothing appears in the meeting.

Does Fireflies have coaching analytics?

Yes, but only on Business ($19 per seat and up) and Enterprise. Conversation Intelligence tracks talk-to-listen ratio, filler words, longest monologue, questions asked, and sentiment, displayed in team dashboards designed for managers coaching their reps. Free and Pro plans get none of it. Work Coach includes its full coaching layer on every plan, and only you can see it.

Can my employer see my Fireflies meetings?

If you are in a company workspace, quite possibly. Meetings can auto-share into channels, Business and Enterprise admins see per-teammate analytics, and the Super Admin role has full visibility into workspace recordings. Work Coach has no workspace, no admin role, and no team plan. Your recordings and coaching are visible only to you, and that is a written commitment in its privacy policy.

What is the best Fireflies alternative for individuals?

It depends on the job. If you want team-style notes without a bot, Granola and Fathom are the usual candidates. If the reason you record is to get better at the meetings themselves, Work Coach is built for exactly that: private, role-aware coaching on every call, with a practice mode to rehearse the hard ones. Keeping Fireflies for team notes and adding Work Coach for coaching is a common setup.

Is the Fireflies free plan really unlimited?

Transcription is unlimited, and that part is real. AI summaries are not: free accounts get a limited number of summary credits (3 to 10 depending on signup source) plus 20 monthly AI credits for AskFred, and storage limits are listed inconsistently in Fireflies' own materials. A common Trustpilot complaint is that the plan feels misleading once the credits run out.
