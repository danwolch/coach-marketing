Plaud vs Otter

Plaud is portable hardware for phone calls and in-person audio. Otter is software for live transcripts and online meeting collaboration.

10 min read · Updated July 2026

The short answer

Pick Plaud when recording happens away from a laptop, especially phone calls, interviews, lectures, medical visits, and in-person conversations across many languages.

Pick Otter when you need a live transcript in Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams, want people to collaborate on notes, and would rather use software you already carry than buy a dedicated recorder.

Pick Work Coach for scheduled desktop meetings when the priority is improving your own delivery, clarity, and influence with private practice after the call.

What Plaud does

Plaud pairs a physical recorder with an AI workspace. Plaud Note and Note Pro handle phone calls and in-person audio. The wearable NotePin models focus on in-person conversations and voice notes. Audio is stored on the device, then synchronized for transcription and analysis.

The app supports 112 languages, more than 10,000 templates, summaries, mind maps, Ask Plaud, and analysis across multiple recordings. The product has an upfront hardware price plus optional paid transcription plans.

What Otter does

Otter is a software meeting assistant. OtterPilot can join calendar meetings, display a live transcript, label speakers, capture slides, summarize the discussion, extract action items, and answer questions. Participants can highlight and comment on the transcript.

Desktop and mobile apps broaden capture beyond the bot. Otter supports a smaller language set than Plaud but fits online collaboration better because the live transcript is available during the meeting.

Capture and platform support

Plaud is designed for physical proximity. A card recorder can attach to a phone for call recording or sit on a table; a pin can be worn during an in-person conversation. This works where meeting bots and desktop audio capture do not, but carrying, charging, and deliberately activating a recorder become part of the workflow.

Otter is designed for software meetings. OtterPilot can autojoin from the calendar, while desktop capture avoids adding a participant. Its mobile app can handle conversations on the go, but it is not the same purpose-built phone-call hardware as Plaud Note.

Notes, search, and workflows

Plaud's advantage appears after varied real-world recordings. Templates can turn audio into interview notes, medical summaries, study materials, or a mind map. Multi-file search helps connect recordings made in different places.

Otter's advantage appears during collaborative work. A live transcript lets people verify wording, mark important passages, comment, and assign action items without waiting for device synchronization. Its shared workspace suits recurring online meetings.

Free plans and pricing

Plaud hardware costs $159 to $189 depending on the model. Starter includes 300 monthly minutes. Pro costs $99.99 yearly or $17.99 monthly for 1,200 minutes; Unlimited costs $239.99 yearly or $29.99 monthly. See Plaud membership pricing.

Otter Basic includes 300 minutes each month with a 30-minute limit per conversation. Pro costs $8.33 per user per month billed annually or $16.99 monthly. Business costs $19.99 annually or $30 monthly. See Otter pricing.

Privacy and AI training

Plaud keeps the initial audio on its device, then uploads it for transcription and AI features. The company lists SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001 and 27701, GDPR, and HIPAA controls. Users should configure cloud retention and protect the physical device.

Otter sends captured meeting content to its cloud workspace and says deidentified data may be used to improve proprietary AI unless the user opts out. Workspace access, recording consent, deletion, and the opt-out setting deserve review before using it for sensitive conversations.

A different option: coaching for the person in the meeting

Both products in this comparison are built to preserve and distribute what happened. That still leaves a personal question unanswered: how well did you handle the conversation?

Frequently asked questions

Is Plaud better than Otter for phone calls?

Plaud Note and Note Pro are purpose-built for phone-call recording, so Plaud is the clearer fit. Always follow local consent law and workplace policy.

Which is better for Zoom, Meet, and Teams?

Otter is generally better because it can join online meetings, produce a live transcript, and support collaboration. Plaud can capture nearby or device audio but has less meeting-platform context.

Do Plaud and Otter have the same free limit?

Both publish a 300-minute monthly allowance, but Plaud's allowance comes with purchased hardware. Otter Basic also imposes a 30-minute limit on each conversation.

Which supports more languages?

Plaud advertises 112 transcription languages. Otter currently lists English, French, Spanish, German, Japanese, and Mandarin.

Does Plaud work without the hardware?

Plaud's core service is tied to a bound device, and Plaud Desktop requires one. Otter is a software subscription and does not require dedicated hardware.
