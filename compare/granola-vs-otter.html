Granola vs Otter.ai

Granola upgrades the notes you type, with nothing visible in the call. Otter sends a bot in and transcribes every word live. The right pick depends on what you want a record of, and who should know it exists.

9 min read · Updated July 2026

The short answer

Pick Granola if you take your own notes and want them quietly upgraded. It captures audio locally, never sends a bot into the call, and turns whatever fragments you typed into polished notes with the transcript as backup. Nobody in the meeting knows it is running.

Pick Otter.ai if you want the full verbatim record. It transcribes live, labels each speaker by name, works on mobile and in a browser, and 300 free minutes a month is enough to test it properly. The trade is a visible bot in your meetings and privacy defaults worth reading about below.

Neither tool tells you how you performed in the meeting. If that is the question you keep asking, there is a third option covered near the end.

What Granola does

Granola calls itself a notepad, and the framing is accurate. You open a note when a meeting starts, type whatever fragments you want, and when the call ends Granola merges your typing with the transcript it captured in the background into clean, complete notes. That transcript comes from local capture of your system audio and microphone, so no bot ever appears in the call and no notification goes to anyone.

It began Mac-only and individual-first, beloved by founders, VCs, and product managers, then expanded to Windows in mid-2025, iOS in early 2025, and Android around July 2026, with in-person recording through the phone apps. Because it captures system audio, it works with Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Slack huddles, and anything else that makes sound. The company raised $125M at a $1.5B valuation in March 2026 and is pushing into enterprise, but the product still centers on one person's notes.

The weak spots are real, though. No video recording, transcription in roughly 17 languages, and speaker labels that default to "Me" and "Them" rather than naming each participant (real names show up only on some platforms, such as Google Meet). Busy group calls can turn into guesswork about who said what.

What Otter.ai does

Otter is one of the oldest and biggest names in meeting transcription, with more than 35 million users and roughly $100M in annual revenue as of 2026. The default flow syncs with your calendar and sends OtterPilot, its bot, into Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams calls as a visible participant. It transcribes live, so you can watch the words appear during the meeting, then delivers a summary with action items.

Its surface area is far bigger than Granola's: a Chrome extension, mobile apps that record in person, Otter AI Chat for questions across your meeting history, integrations with Slack, Salesforce, HubSpot, and Notion, plus a 2025 suite of agents aimed at sales and recruiting teams. A desktop app added in December 2025 can record without sending the bot in, though the bot remains the primary experience.

Transcription accuracy has a strong reputation on clear audio and a weaker one with accents and crosstalk, a criticism Granola shares.

The bot question

This is the fork in the road, and it runs deeper than convenience.

Granola shows nothing to anyone. No bot in the participant list, no notification, no recording banner. An optional chat message for disclosure exists, but sending it is your call. That invisibility is exactly what many users want in client calls and interviews, where a bot changes the room. It also means consent is entirely your responsibility, and the ethics of silent capture are a recurring debate about Granola on Reddit and Hacker News. Many jurisdictions require all-party consent to record.

Otter wears its recording openly. OtterPilot appears as a participant everyone can see, which functions as its own notification. The complication is that the bot often shows up where nobody wanted it: uninvited joins through calendar sync are the most widely reported Otter complaint, to the point that a security vendor published a guide on blocking it. The 2025 class action against Otter argues the consent flow still falls short, since consent is sought only from hosts who are not Otter users and participants cannot decline.

The honest framing: Granola gives you full control and puts the legal burden on you. Otter gives everyone visibility and takes some control away from you.

Notes vs transcripts

Granola's output is your notes, improved. Type three bullet fragments during the call and you get back a structured note that reads like you wrote it with full attention, with the transcript underneath when you need to check a quote. Custom templates shape the output, and an AI chat answers questions within and across meetings. People who already take notes tend to love this. People who want to stop taking notes entirely find it odd, because the product works best when you type something.

Otter's output is the record itself: a complete searchable transcript with named speakers, a generated summary, action items, and AI chat across everything you have recorded. You never need to type. For teams this compounds into a shared archive, and the Business plan layers admin controls and channel sharing on top. If a dispute ever comes down to exactly who said what, Otter has the evidence and Granola often has "Them."

Privacy and AI training

Two different postures. Granola stores no audio at all: sound streams out for transcription and is deleted immediately afterward. Notes and transcripts live in Granola's cloud, transcripts pass to third-party language models under no-training agreements, and you can opt out of Granola training its own models on your data. It earned SOC 2 Type 2 in July 2025.

Otter trains its models on de-identified customer conversations by default, opt-out rather than opt-in. That practice, along with how OtterPilot handles consent, sits at the center of Brewer v. Otter.ai, a class action filed in August 2025 and still working through motions in 2026. Otter holds SOC 2 certification and offers admin controls on Business and Enterprise, where the workspace belongs to the company rather than to you, so what colleagues and admins can see depends on your settings.

Pricing

Granola's free Basic plan includes unlimited meetings and AI notes, with the catch that history rolls off after about 30 days. Keeping your archive requires Business at $14 per user per month, which also adds advanced AI models, integrations, and an API; Enterprise runs $35 with SSO and admin governance. Current details are on Granola's pricing page.

Otter's free plan gives you 300 transcription minutes a month, capped at 30 minutes per conversation. Pro is $8.33 per user per month billed annually ($16.99 monthly) and raises the ceiling to 1,200 minutes. Business, at $19.99 annually ($30 monthly), removes the monthly cap and adds admin controls, per Otter's pricing page.

Rough math for one person: free Granola works if 30 days of history is enough, and free Otter works if you meet fewer than five hours a month. Past that, Otter Pro is the cheapest full-transcript option and Granola Business is the cheapest way to keep botless notes forever.

A different option if the meetings are about you

Both of these tools produce a record for later. Neither evaluates how you actually did.

On analytics, the precise state of things: Granola has none at all, no talk-time ratios, no filler counts, no coaching layer of any kind. Otter shows a talk-time percentage breakdown on paid plans, and its real coaching features (live prompts, talk-listen ratios, objection tracking) sit inside the Sales Agent, which is Enterprise-only and built for sales managers reviewing their reps. Nothing in either product gives an individual private feedback on their own communication.

Work Coach exists for that gap. It captures meetings the way Granola does, locally on your Mac or Windows machine with no bot and nothing visible to other participants, but it does a different job with the audio. It classifies the meeting and your role in it, then scores the dimensions that matter for that role with timestamped evidence you can play back. It counts your filler words per minute and plays each one so you hear yourself. It analyzes the pitch, pace, and tone of your voice in high-stakes moments. And it generates practice roleplays from your real meetings, so you can rerun a flubbed conversation before the next one counts.

The price is $50 a month after a two-week free trial with no credit card, set against coaching rather than note-taking (a human executive coach runs about $5,000 a month). There is no team workspace and no admin console, and the privacy policy commits in writing that your employer can never see any of it. Keep Granola or Otter for the notes; Work Coach watches the same meetings and works only for you.

  • Refuse to send a bot into client calls or interviews
  • Meet on anything with audio, including huddles and calls Otter's bot can't join
  • Record in person from your phone

Pick Otter.ai if you...

  • Want complete verbatim transcripts with named speakers
  • Like live transcription you can watch during the call

  • Want a free plan you can use for months of light meetings
  • Accept a visible bot and default AI training in exchange

Frequently asked questions

Which is better, Granola or Otter.ai?

It depends on what you want the record to be. Granola is better if you take your own notes and want them quietly improved, with no bot in the call and nothing visible to anyone else. Otter is better if you want a complete searchable transcript, live transcription during the call, mobile recording, and a free tier you can test properly. Teams that need shared archives usually land on Otter; individuals who mostly need their own notes usually prefer Granola.

Does Granola notify people that it is recording?

No. Granola captures system audio locally and shows nothing to other participants. An optional chat message for disclosure exists, but sending it is up to you, and recording laws in many places require all-party consent. Otter's bot is the opposite: it appears in the participant list, which acts as its own notification, although the 2025 class action argues its consent flow still falls short.

Does Otter train its AI on my conversations?

Yes. As of mid-2026 Otter trains its models on de-identified customer conversations by default, and a class action filed in 2025 challenges the practice. Granola takes a different posture: audio is deleted right after transcription, transcripts go to third-party models under no-training agreements, and you can opt out of Granola training its own models on your data.

Can I use Granola or Otter for free?

Both have real free tiers with different catches. Granola's free plan includes unlimited meetings and AI notes but keeps only roughly the last 30 days of history. Otter's free plan gives you 300 transcription minutes a month with a 30-minute cap per conversation. Light users can live on either; regular users hit the limits within weeks.

Is there an alternative that coaches you instead of taking notes?

Work Coach records locally with no bot, the same capture approach as Granola, but analyzes how you communicated instead of writing notes for the team. You get role-aware coaching scores, filler words per minute with playback, voice delivery analysis, and a practice mode, all private to you. It costs $50 a month after a two-week free trial, and your employer can never see any of it.
