Work Coach vs Granola

Granola and Work Coach record meetings the same way: locally, on your computer, with no bot. Granola turns that capture into better notes. Work Coach turns it into private coaching. Here is how to choose.

10 min read · Updated July 2026

The short answer

Pick Granola if you already take notes during meetings and want them to come out better. It records locally with no bot, merges the bullets you type with the transcript, and its free Basic plan covers unlimited meetings. As a notes tool for individuals, it may be the best-liked product in the category.

Pick Work Coach if the meetings you care about are the ones where your performance matters: interviews, 1:1s, sales calls, presentations. It captures audio the same botless way, then coaches you on what it heard: role-specific scores, filler words per minute, voice delivery analysis, and a practice mode. Granola has no coaching layer at all.

The two share capture DNA and almost nothing else. Plenty of people run both.

What Granola does

Granola calls itself a notepad, and the framing is accurate. You open a note when a meeting starts, type whatever rough fragments you want during the call, and when it ends Granola merges your typing with the transcript into a clean, structured summary. The bullets you wrote shape what the AI emphasizes, which is why people who already take notes tend to love it.

Capture is local and botless. Granola listens to system audio and your microphone on-device, so nothing joins the call and nothing is visible to other participants. It started Mac-only and built a devoted following among founders, VCs, and product managers before expanding to Windows in 2025, iPhone in early 2025, and Android in mid-2026. The phone apps record in-person conversations too.

Around the notepad, Granola has added custom templates, AI chat within and across meetings, shared folders, and integrations with Slack, Notion, and HubSpot, plus an API and MCP server on paid tiers. The company raised a $125M Series C at a $1.5B valuation in March 2026 and is now pushing into enterprise, positioning itself as meeting memory for companies.

What Work Coach does

Work Coach records the same way Granola does: locally, with no bot, invisible to everyone else on the call. The overlap ends there, because Work Coach is not trying to produce notes. It is trying to make you better at the meeting itself.

After each conversation you get the table stakes, a timestamped transcript and an editable summary. The reason to open the app is what sits on top:

  • Role-aware coaching. Work Coach classifies the meeting (interview, 1:1, standup, sales call, presentation, and more) and your role in it, then scores the dimensions that matter for that role. An interviewee gets scored on STAR answer quality. A manager in a 1:1 gets scored on psychological safety and accountability. Every score comes with timestamped evidence.
  • Filler words, counted honestly. A deterministic counter (not an AI guess) tracks your ums, likes, and you-knows per minute, flags whether you are in a low or excessive band, and plays each instance back so you hear yourself.
  • Voice delivery. It picks up to five high-stakes moments of your own speech and analyzes the audio itself: pitch, pace, pauses, projection, and tone, scored out of 100 with specific suggestions.
  • Practice. Live voice roleplay with an AI playing the other person, generated from your real meetings. Flubbed an answer today? Rerun the exchange tonight and get scored on the rep.
  • A coach that remembers. Cross-meeting patterns, private notes on the people you work with, and a coach chat that can answer "did I talk too much?" with the exact moment queued up.

There is no team product hiding behind any of this. No shared folders, no CRM sync, no enterprise tier. The product is you, improving.

How they record your meetings

This is the section where the two agree. Neither sends a bot. Neither notifies participants. Both capture system audio plus your microphone directly on your machine, which means both work with Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, Slack huddles, and anything else that makes sound on your computer. With either tool, telling people you record is your call and your responsibility, the same as with any local recorder. Granola offers an optional chat message to announce recording; Work Coach never auto-records and instead prompts you before each capture, so nothing is taped without you pressing start.

Speaker labeling deserves an honest note, because the two share the same limitation. Granola's default transcript labels are Me and Them: your mic is Me, everything arriving through system audio is Them. Real participant names appear only on Google Meet (Mac and Windows) and Zoom (Mac), and Reddit threads about Granola regularly flag who-said-what confusion in group calls. Work Coach labels the same way, you versus everyone else, and does not name individual colleagues either. Its coaching sidesteps most of the problem because feedback is grounded only in your own speech, which is always identified. But if you need every voice in a six-person call named reliably, neither tool is built for that.

Two real differences remain. Granola records in-person conversations through its phone apps; Work Coach is desktop-only with no in-person mode. In the other direction, Granola deletes audio immediately after transcription, so there is nothing to play back later. Work Coach keeps audio on your device for 30 days, and that playback is load-bearing: every piece of coaching links to the actual moment so you can hear yourself say it.

What you get after the meeting

From Granola: notes that read the way you would have written them with more time. The enhanced-notes trick genuinely works, templates adapt the output to the meeting type, and the AI chat can answer questions across your history. What you will not find is any analysis of how the conversation went. No talk-time ratios, no filler-word counts, no pace feedback, no coaching dashboards, on any plan. Granola tells you what happened; it has no opinion about how you did.

From Work Coach: the opinion is the product. It classifies the meeting and your role, then scores what matters for that role: an interviewee's STAR answers, a seller's discovery depth, a manager's follow-through in a 1:1. It counts your filler words per minute and plays every instance back. It analyzes the audio of your highest-stakes moments for pitch, pace, pauses, projection, and tone. And when a conversation goes sideways, practice mode builds a voice roleplay from it, which earns its keep when you are working up to something like a promotion ask.

If your reaction to Granola is that you love the capture method but wish it told you something about yourself, that gap is exactly where Work Coach lives.

Privacy and who sees your data

Granola's story here is more careful than most note-takers. Audio streams to its transcription provider and is deleted immediately after transcription; no recordings are stored. Notes and transcripts live in Granola's cloud, transcripts pass to third-party LLMs (OpenAI, Anthropic) under no-training agreements, and you can opt out of Granola's own model training, though the org-wide opt-out is an Enterprise feature. Granola earned SOC 2 Type 2 in 2025. The recurring criticism on Reddit and Hacker News is about silent capture ethics rather than data handling, and that criticism applies to any local recorder, Work Coach included.

Work Coach adds commitments aimed at one specific fear: your employer. Meeting audio stays on your computer and auto-deletes after 30 days; transcripts and coaching feedback live in your private account; and the privacy policy states in writing that your data is never shared with your employer, your manager, or HR, and never trains shared models. No admin console or team dashboard exists that could expose your meetings, and your coaching history stays with you if you change jobs. As Granola moves upmarket toward enterprise deployments with admin controls, that structural difference is worth watching.

Pricing

Granola's free Basic plan is generous in one direction and tight in another: unlimited meetings and AI notes, but your history rolls off after roughly 30 days. As of mid-2026 the old individual paid plan has been retired, so keeping notes past a month means Business at $14 per user per month, which also adds advanced AI models, integrations, and API access. Enterprise, at $35, adds SSO and admin controls. Current details are on Granola's pricing page.

Work Coach costs $50 a month after a two-week free trial with no credit card. That is more than Granola because it is priced against a different alternative: a traditional executive coach runs about $5,000 a month, and Work Coach delivers the feedback loop at 1% of that. Higher tiers add humans, with async coach review of your progress at $250 a month and a live monthly session at $500.

If notes are the whole job, Granola is cheaper and excellent. If how you performed is the job, the comparison stops being about price.

Can you use both together?

Yes, and nothing conflicts. Both record locally and independently, so you can keep typing notes into Granola while Work Coach captures the same meeting for coaching. Neither is aware of the other.

For meetings that only exist as Granola transcripts, Work Coach can import them: paste the transcript and get a coaching pass after the fact, though without audio there is no voice analysis or playback.

Frequently asked questions

Does Granola use a bot to record meetings?

No. Granola captures system audio and your microphone locally on your device when you open a note, and nothing is visible to other participants. Work Coach records the same botless way. The difference is what each does with the capture: Granola turns it into enhanced notes shaped by what you typed, while Work Coach turns it into private coaching on how you communicated.

Does Granola have speaker analytics or coaching?

No. Granola has no talk-time ratios, filler-word counts, pace analysis, or coaching features on any plan. The closest workaround is asking its AI chat questions about your transcripts. Work Coach is built around that missing layer: role-aware coaching scores, filler words per minute with audio playback, voice delivery analysis, and a practice mode.

What is a good Granola alternative for interview or 1:1 feedback?

Work Coach uses the same botless local capture as Granola but analyzes your performance instead of producing notes. It classifies the meeting and your role, then scores what matters: STAR answer quality if you are the interviewee, psychological safety and accountability if you are the manager in a 1:1, with each piece of feedback linked to audio of the actual moment.

How long does Granola keep my meetings on the free plan?

As of mid-2026, Granola's free Basic plan limits your meeting history to a rolling window of roughly 30 days. Unlimited history requires the Business plan at $14 per user per month. Work Coach works differently: meeting audio auto-deletes from your device after 30 days, but your transcripts, coaching feedback, and progress history stay in your private account for as long as you keep it.

Can I use Granola and Work Coach together?

Yes. Both record locally and independently, so nothing conflicts. Keep typing notes into Granola while Work Coach captures the same meeting for coaching. You can also paste a Granola transcript into Work Coach to get coaching on a meeting it did not record, though without audio there is no voice analysis or playback.
