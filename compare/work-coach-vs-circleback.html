Work Coach vs Circleback

Circleback turns your meetings into the cleanest structured notes in the category. Work Coach watches the same meetings and coaches you on how you ran them. Here is where each one earns its price.

10 min read · Updated July 2026

The short answer

Pick Circleback if what you want from a meeting tool is the notes. Its structured summaries and auto-assigned action items are widely regarded as the best in the category, and its automations push them into Slack, Notion, or your CRM without you touching anything. There is no free plan, but at $20.83 a month billed annually it is priced like the premium notetaker it is.

Pick Work Coach if the meetings you care about are interviews, 1:1s, and sales calls where how you performed matters more than what got written down. It records locally with no bot, then coaches you: role-aware scores, filler words per minute with playback, voice delivery analysis, and a practice mode. Circleback has none of that on any plan.

The two do different jobs on the same audio, and running both is a reasonable setup.

What Circleback does

Circleback is a small, focused company (Y Combinator W24, founded 2023, roughly ten people) that has built an outsized reputation on one thing: the quality of its notes. Instead of a wall of transcript, you get a structured overview with action items automatically extracted and assigned to the right people. Reviewers who have churned through half a dozen notetakers regularly land on Circleback for exactly this reason.

Capture is flexible. A meeting bot can join Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, or Webex calls, visible in the participant list and posting a chat message that discloses the recording. Or you can skip the bot entirely: a desktop app for Mac and Windows records mic and system audio locally with nothing visible in the call. Mobile apps for iOS and Android handle in-person conversations, and since June 2026 there is even an Apple Watch app.

Around the notes sits a serious automation layer. Post-meeting workflows send notes and action items to Slack, Notion, HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio, Linear, and Asana, with webhooks, Zapier, and Make covering everything else. A July 2026 release added App Connectors, which let its AI search and act across those connected tools. Transcription covers 100+ languages, and every plan includes unlimited meetings.

Its privacy posture is strong for the category too: SOC 2 Type II, GDPR compliance, and a plain statement that customer data is never used to train models.

What Work Coach does

Work Coach listens to the same meetings and asks a different question: how did you do? It is a desktop app for Mac and Windows that records locally, with no bot, and then analyzes your side of the conversation.

You still get the table stakes, a timestamped transcript and an editable summary with action items. The product is what comes after:

  • Role-aware coaching scores. It classifies the meeting (interview, 1:1, sales call, standup, and eight more types) and your role in it, then scores the dimensions that matter: STAR answer quality if you were the candidate, psychological safety if you were the manager, discovery depth if you were selling. Every score comes with timestamped evidence you can play back.
  • Filler words, counted exactly. A deterministic counter tracks your ums, likes, and you-knows per minute, tells you which band you fall in, and plays every instance back so you hear yourself.
  • Voice delivery. Up to five high-stakes moments of your own speech get analyzed as audio, on pitch, pace, pauses, projection, and tone, scored out of 100 with specific suggestions.
  • Practice mode. Live voice roleplay with an AI playing the other side, built from your real meetings, with scored reps and trend lines across sessions.
  • A coach that remembers. Cross-meeting patterns, private notes on the people you work with, and a coach chat that answers questions grounded in your actual conversations.

There is no sharing, no CRM sync, and no team workspace, on purpose. The output goes to exactly one person.

How they record your meetings

This part of the comparison is closer than most, because Circleback took botless capture seriously.

Its default flow still sends a bot into Zoom, Meet, Teams, or Webex calls. The bot appears in the participant list and posts a chat message disclosing the recording, which is the most consent-friendly approach in this space, and also the most conspicuous. For calls where a bot would change the tone, the Circleback desktop app records locally on Mac or Windows with nothing visible, auto-detecting when a meeting app starts using your microphone.

Work Coach works only the second way. It sits in your menu bar, notices when a meeting app goes live, and asks whether you want to record. Nothing ever appears in the call, and it never records without you pressing start. Because capture is local system audio, it covers Zoom, Meet, Teams, Slack huddles, Webex, FaceTime, and anything else your computer plays. As with any local recorder, telling people you record is your call and your responsibility.

Where Circleback clearly wins: coverage. Phone apps and the Apple Watch mean hallway conversations and coffee chats can become notes too. Work Coach is desktop-only, with no mobile app and no in-person mode. Circleback also identifies each speaker by name, while Work Coach separates you (microphone) from everyone else (system audio) and coaches only your side, without naming your colleagues in the transcript.

What you get after the meeting

From Circleback: the best version of what every notetaker promises. A clean structured summary, action items with owners, a searchable history, an AI you can ask about past meetings, and automations that file everything where it belongs. If your pain is note-taking and follow-up, that pain largely ends.

From Work Coach: feedback. Where Circleback records that you committed to a revised proposal by Friday, Work Coach points out that you made that commitment after two minutes of hedging, plays you the clip, and suggests how to give a clean answer next time. It scores whether you set an agenda and whether the meeting ended with clear next steps, and its practice mode lets you rerun the hard exchange before the stakes are real again.

On analytics there is no overlap to weigh. Circleback has no talk-time, filler-word, sentiment, or coaching features on any plan; its own comparison pages send people who want coaching insights to other tools while it competes on transcript quality and automation. That focus is a strength, and it also means Circleback will never tell you anything about how you come across. If you are working on running better 1:1s with your manager or heading into a job search where every interview is a performance, that missing layer is the whole point.

Notes tell you what happened. A coach tells you what to fix.

Privacy and who sees your data

Both products have clean stories here, which is refreshing for the category.

Circleback is SOC 2 Type II audited, GDPR compliant, certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, and states plainly that customer data is never used to train models. Meetings are private to you by default, with sharing controls you manage. On the Team plan the calculus shifts a little: shared meetings, team-wide AI search, admin-enforced settings, and a usage dashboard exist because teams want them, so what colleagues and admins can see depends on how the workspace is configured.

Work Coach removes the question entirely. There is no team plan, no admin role, and no workspace. Meeting audio stays on your computer and is deleted automatically after 30 days; no audio files are stored in the cloud. Transcripts and coaching live in your private account, with a written commitment that your employer, your manager, and HR cannot see them and that your data never trains shared models. If you change jobs, your coaching history goes with you.

Pricing

Circleback has no free plan, only a 7-day trial. Individual costs $25 a month, or $20.83 a month billed annually. Team is $30 per user monthly or $25 annually, adding shared meetings, team automations, and admin controls, with custom Enterprise pricing above that. Every plan includes unlimited meetings. Current details are on Circleback's pricing page. The most common gripe about it is exactly this: it costs more than Otter or Fireflies and gives away nothing for free.

Work Coach is $50 a month after a two-week trial that needs no credit card. That is more than double Circleback, and the honest comparison is a different one: an executive coach runs about $5,000 a month, and Work Coach does the observation-and-feedback half of that job for one percent of the price. Tiers at $250 and $500 add a human coach, reviewing your progress async or meeting you live each month.

Can you use both together?

Comfortably, yes. Both can record locally at the same time without interfering, or you can let Circleback's bot handle the call while Work Coach captures your side quietly. Circleback files the notes and action items for the team; Work Coach debriefs you in private.

Work Coach can also import transcripts from other tools, so a meeting that only exists in Circleback can still get a coaching pass afterward.

Which one should you pick?

Pick Circleback if you...

  • Want the best structured notes money currently buys
  • Push meeting outcomes into Slack, Notion, or a CRM automatically
  • Record in person via phone or Apple Watch
  • Need every speaker identified by name
  • Value a notetaker with a no-training privacy policy

Pick Work Coach if you...

  • Want feedback on how you communicate, not just a record of it
  • Have interviews, 1:1s, or sales calls where your performance is the point
  • Want to practice hard conversations before the stakes are real
  • Care about patterns over time, not one meeting's notes
  • Need certainty that your employer never sees any of it

Frequently asked questions

Does Circleback have a free plan?

No. Circleback offers a 7-day free trial, then paid plans start at $20.83 per month billed annually ($25 monthly) for Individual. It is one of the few notetakers with no permanent free tier. Work Coach also has no free tier; it offers a 2-week trial with no credit card, then costs $50 a month.

Does Circleback record without a bot?

Yes. Alongside its meeting bot, Circleback has a desktop app for Mac and Windows that records mic and system audio locally with nothing visible in the call, plus mobile apps and an Apple Watch app for in-person conversations. Work Coach is botless only: local capture on Mac or Windows, started by you, with nothing shown to other participants.

Does Circleback have coaching or talk-time analytics?

No. Circleback has no talk-time percentages, filler-word tracking, sentiment analysis, or coaching features on any plan; it competes on note quality and automations, and its own comparison pages point people who want coaching toward other tools. Work Coach is built around that layer: role-aware coaching scores, filler words per minute with audio playback, voice delivery analysis, and a practice mode.

Can my employer see my Circleback meetings?

On the Individual plan, meetings are private by default and you control sharing. On the Team plan, shared meetings, team-wide AI search, and admin-enforced settings mean visibility depends on how the workspace is configured. Work Coach has no workspace or admin role at all; your recordings, transcripts, and coaching feedback are visible only to you.

Can I use Circleback and Work Coach together?

Yes. They can record the same meeting independently, and neither interferes with the other. Circleback produces the notes and pushes them into your tools; Work Coach gives you private coaching on how you communicated. Work Coach can also import transcripts, so meetings it did not record can still get a coaching pass.
