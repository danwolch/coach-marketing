Work Coach vs Jamie

Jamie is the privacy purist of note-takers: no bot, no video, and your audio is deleted the moment it is transcribed. Work Coach is private in a different way, and it coaches you. Here is how to choose.

10 min read · Updated July 2026

The short answer

Pick Jamie if you want a bot-free note-taker with the strictest audio handling in the category. It records from your desktop with nothing visible in the meeting, transcribes in Frankfurt, permanently deletes the audio afterward, and never trains AI models on your conversations. For consultants, lawyers, and executives who cannot have a bot or a stored recording anywhere, that design is the whole point.

Pick Work Coach if you want to get better at the meetings rather than only document them. It is also bot-free and records locally, but it keeps your audio on your own computer for 30 days, which is what makes coaching possible: playing back the moment you buried your recommendation, analyzing how your voice actually sounded, counting your filler words. Jamie has no analytics or coaching of any kind.

The two products agree completely about bots. They disagree about what should happen after the meeting ends.

What Jamie does

Jamie is a bot-free AI note-taker from a bootstrapped company in Cologne, Germany, founded in 2022 and profitable enough to stay independent (roughly $2.4M in annual revenue and about 31 employees as of 2026). Its desktop app for Mac and Windows captures system audio and your microphone directly, so nothing joins the call and other participants see nothing. It works with any meeting software, including phone calls routed through your computer, and an iOS app (with Android in beta) records in-person conversations by microphone.

After each meeting Jamie produces structured notes with decisions and action items, shaped by templates you can customize. Speaker recognition names people and remembers them across meetings, and an assistant sidebar (Ctrl + J) answers questions about your meetings from anywhere on your desktop. Notes export to Notion, Google Docs, and OneNote on every plan, with Salesforce, HubSpot, and Asana on Pro.

The reason people choose Jamie, though, is its data handling. Audio is uploaded, transcribed in Frankfurt on serverless infrastructure that stores nothing, and then permanently deleted. Transcripts stay on German servers under GDPR, the company holds ISO 27001 certification, and neither Jamie nor its model providers train AI on your data. Among note-takers, this is the strongest privacy story going, and Jamie has earned its reputation with a bot-averse professional crowd.

There are real trade-offs. Jamie captures no video at all. Deleted audio means you can never re-listen to a meeting, so if the transcript got something wrong, the record is gone. And Trustpilot reviewers complain about bugs at a premium price, including meetings lost during app upgrades and missed participants on large calls.

What Work Coach does

Work Coach shares Jamie's starting point, local bot-free capture on your desktop, and then heads somewhere Jamie deliberately does not go. It is a private AI coach for Mac and Windows that watches the meetings you already have and gives you feedback on how you communicated.

You still get the table stakes: a timestamped transcript and an editable summary with action items. The product, though, is the coaching stacked on top:

  • Role-aware coaching scores. Work Coach classifies each meeting (interview, 1:1, standup, sales call, presentation, and more) and your role in it, then scores the dimensions that matter for that role, with timestamped evidence. An interviewee gets scored on STAR answer quality; a manager in a 1:1 gets scored on psychological safety.
  • Hear the moment. Every insight has a button that plays the actual audio behind the feedback, so you hear yourself hedge, rush, or land the point.
  • Filler words, counted deterministically. Your ums, likes, and you-knows per minute, banded from low to excessive, with each instance played back in sequence.
  • Voice delivery. Up to five high-stakes moments of your own speech analyzed as audio: pitch, pace, pause, power, and tone, scored out of 100.
  • Practice. Live voice roleplay with an AI playing the other person, generated from your real meetings, with scored reps and trends.
  • A coach with memory. Cross-meeting patterns, private notes on the people you work with, and a coach chat grounded in your actual conversations.

Nothing is shared with a team, because there is no team layer. No CRM sync, no admin console, no seats. The product is you, improving.

How they record your meetings

For once, both sides of a comparison agree on the big question. No bot ever joins the call. Jamie captures system audio and your microphone through its desktop app; Work Coach does the same through a menu bar app on Mac or Windows. Neither appears in the participant list, neither notifies anyone, and in both cases telling people you record is your call and your responsibility, the same as with any local recorder.

The differences sit at the edges. Jamie also records in person through its mobile apps and syncs across devices, while Work Coach is desktop-only. Work Coach notices when a meeting app starts using your microphone and asks whether to record; it never starts on its own. Jamie names each speaker and remembers them from meeting to meeting, while Work Coach separates you (mic) from everyone else (system audio) without naming individual colleagues. Neither product records video.

Then comes the fork. The moment transcription finishes, Jamie destroys the audio forever. Work Coach keeps it on your machine for 30 days, then deletes it automatically. Everything else in this comparison flows from that one design decision.

What you get after the meeting

From Jamie: clean structured notes, decisions, action items, and a transcript you can question from the sidebar. As a personal record of what happened, it does the job well, and its named-speaker notes are genuinely better for minutes than Work Coach's you-versus-everyone labeling.

From Work Coach: a transcript and summary too, and then the part Jamie does not attempt. Coaching scores tuned to your role in the meeting, each claim backed by a timestamped quote. A filler-word count with a per-minute rate. A voice delivery score built from the audio of your highest-stakes moments. An offer to rerun the conversation in practice mode until the weak answer comes out clean.

Jamie's own roadmap discussions treat speaker analytics as a gap: no talk-time stats, no monologue lengths, no filler-word counts, on any plan. Part of that follows from its privacy design. Once the audio is gone, no product on earth could tell you how your voice sounded or play the moment back. Work Coach keeps audio local for 30 days precisely so it can do both: every insight has a Hear the moment button, and the voice analysis works from the sound itself rather than the words alone.

If your goal for the year is to speak up more in meetings or run better 1:1s with your manager, that playback loop is the mechanism that makes the change stick. Notes cannot do it, however well written.

Privacy and who sees your data

Jamie's privacy design deserves the credit it gets. Audio goes to Frankfurt, is transcribed on serverless GPUs that store nothing, and is then permanently deleted. Transcripts live on German servers with AES encryption under GDPR. The company is ISO 27001 certified, with SOC 2 Type II pending as of mid-2026, and it commits that neither it nor its model providers train on your data. If your threat model says a stored recording anywhere, even on your own laptop, is unacceptable, Jamie deletes soonest, and it wins this section for you.

Work Coach answers the same worry with a different architecture. Meeting audio never leaves your computer as a stored file; it stays on your device and deletes itself after 30 days. Transcripts and coaching are processed and stored securely in your private account, and the privacy policy commits in writing that your data is never used to train shared models, never shared with other users, and never visible to your employer, your manager, or HR. There is no admin console or team dashboard that could expose it, and your coaching history stays with you if you change jobs.

The employer question is worth a pause. Jamie's Team plan shares notes across 2 to 10 seats, a modest team layer with no heavy admin machinery. Work Coach has no team concept at all, which matters when the recording is your interview prep or a salary negotiation. Both products leave recording consent entirely with you, so know your local rules before you hit record with either.

Pricing

Jamie prices in euros only, on every plan. The free tier covers 10 meetings a month with a 30-minute cap per meeting. Plus is €21 per month billed annually (€25 monthly) for 20 meetings a month with a 2-hour cap. Pro is €39 annually (€47 monthly) for unlimited meetings with a 3-hour cap plus CRM integrations, and Team runs €33 per seat annually for 2 to 10 seats. Current numbers are on Jamie's pricing page; US buyers should note the dollar cost moves with the exchange rate. Reviewers who compare it with cheaper transcription tools tend to call Jamie expensive for what is, in the end, a note-taker.

Work Coach costs $50 a month after a two-week free trial that needs no credit card. That is more than Jamie's Pro tier because it is priced as coaching rather than note-taking: the benchmark is an executive coach at $5,000 a month, not a transcript archive. Higher tiers add humans, with async review of your progress by a real coach at $250 a month and a monthly live session at $500.

Can you use both together?

Yes, and the pairing is unusually clean because neither sends a bot into your calls. Let Jamie keep writing the notes, the decisions, and the action items with named speakers, while Work Coach handles the private debrief on how you performed.

Work Coach can also import transcripts, so a meeting that only exists as a Jamie transcript can still get a coaching pass after the fact. Audio-based features like voice delivery and playback need Work Coach to have recorded the meeting itself.

Which one should you pick?

Pick Jamie if you...

  • Need notes with zero retained audio, deleted right after transcription
  • Want EU data residency under GDPR, stored in Frankfurt
  • Record in person on mobile as well as on desktop
  • Want named speakers remembered across meetings
  • Push notes to Notion, Google Docs, or a CRM

Pick Work Coach if you...

  • Want feedback on how you communicate, not only a record of it
  • Want to re-listen to key moments instead of losing the audio forever
  • Care about voice delivery: pace, pitch, pauses, projection
  • Want to practice hard conversations before the stakes are real
  • Need certainty that your employer never sees any of it

Frequently asked questions

Does Jamie use a bot to join meetings?

No. Jamie captures system audio and your microphone directly through its desktop app, so nothing appears in the participant list. Work Coach records the same way, locally on your Mac or Windows machine. Neither product notifies other participants, so telling people you record stays your responsibility with both.

Does Jamie keep recordings of my meetings?

No. Audio is uploaded, transcribed in Frankfurt, and then permanently deleted; only the transcript and notes are stored. That is excellent data minimization, but it means you can never re-listen to a meeting. Work Coach keeps audio on your own computer for 30 days, then auto-deletes it, so you can play back the moments behind its coaching.

Does Jamie have speaker analytics or coaching?

No. Jamie has no talk-time stats, no monologue or filler-word counts, and no coaching features on any plan; speaker analytics is a documented gap. Work Coach is built around that layer: role-aware coaching scores, filler words per minute with audio playback, voice delivery analysis, and a practice mode.

Can I use Jamie and Work Coach together?

Yes. Both record locally without a bot, and they do different jobs: Jamie writes the notes and action items, while Work Coach coaches how you communicated. You can also paste a transcript from Jamie into Work Coach to get coaching on a meeting Work Coach did not record.

How much does Jamie cost?

Jamie's free plan covers 10 meetings a month with a 30-minute cap. Plus is €21 per month billed annually for 20 meetings with a 2-hour cap, and Pro is €39 per month billed annually for unlimited meetings. All pricing is in euros. Work Coach costs $50 a month after a 2-week free trial.
