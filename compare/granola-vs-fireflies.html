Granola vs Fireflies

Granola is a focused bot-free notebook for the person in the meeting. Fireflies is a broad recording and automation system for teams.

10 min read · Updated July 2026

The short answer

Pick Granola when you want a quiet, bot-free workflow that turns your own rough notes into a clean document and does not retain meeting audio.

Pick Fireflies when you need 100+ languages, multiple capture methods, replayable recordings, CRM automation, and analytics across a team.

Pick Work Coach if your main question after a meeting is how you can communicate more clearly and effectively next time.

What Granola does

Granola is a desktop meeting notebook. It captures the user's microphone and system audio without joining as a participant, combines the transcript with any rough notes typed during the call, and produces an editable document in a chosen template.

Audio is discarded after transcription. Granola therefore feels less like call surveillance and more like a personal note-taking aid, although the transcript and generated note still need careful sharing controls.

What Fireflies does

Fireflies records and transcribes through a visible Fred bot, a bot-free Google Meet extension, desktop and mobile apps, dialers, or file upload. It generates summaries, topics, action items, soundbites, speaker analytics, and answers across a call library.

Its integrations and Business-tier conversation intelligence make it useful for sales, recruiting, customer success, and operations teams that want meeting data to flow into other systems.

Capture and platform support

Granola has one clear interaction model: open the desktop app, start a note, and let it hear the meeting without adding a participant. That is appealing for 1:1s and internal meetings where a bot would feel heavy.

Fireflies wins on coverage. Fred can autojoin calendar meetings, while other capture methods cover Google Meet without a bot, phone calls, in-person conversations, desktop audio, and existing files. The tradeoff is a more complex product and a cloud recording library.

Notes, search, and workflows

Granola treats the user's typed cues as editorial direction. The final note is compact, editable, and suitable for collaborative documents. It does not aim to be a compliance archive or a revenue-intelligence database.

Fireflies keeps more source material and attaches automation to it. Users can search transcripts, play soundbites, track topics, ask AI questions, measure talk patterns, and push outputs into a CRM. This is much more capable for operations, but often more than one person needs.

Free plans and pricing

Granola Free allows unlimited meetings with limited recent history. Business costs $14 per user per month, while Enterprise is $35. See Granola pricing.

Fireflies Free advertises unlimited transcription with limits around AI summaries and advanced features. Pro costs $10 per user per month billed annually or $18 monthly. Business is $19 annually or $29 monthly and adds conversation intelligence. See Fireflies pricing.

Privacy and AI training

Granola says it deletes meeting audio after transcription and contracts with AI providers not to train on customer data. It may improve its own systems with customer data unless the account opts out.

Fireflies says neither it nor its AI vendors train models on customer meeting content. It lists SOC 2 Type II and GDPR controls. Because Fireflies stores recordings and connects to business systems, admins should configure consent, retention, access, and integration scope.

A different option: coaching for the person in the meeting

Both products in this comparison are built to preserve and distribute what happened. That still leaves a personal question unanswered: how well did you handle the conversation?

Work Coach records locally on Mac or Windows with no bot, then coaches only the account owner. It applies role-aware rubrics, links feedback to timestamped evidence, counts filler words with playback, reviews voice delivery, and turns real moments into voice practice. The results never appear in a manager dashboard or shared meeting report. It costs $50 a month after a two-week free trial.

Frequently asked questions

Does Granola or Fireflies add a meeting bot?

Granola does not. Fireflies uses the visible Fred bot by default for calendar meetings, but also offers bot-free capture through its Google Meet extension and other apps.

Which stores the meeting recording?

Fireflies can keep a replayable recording. Granola says it deletes audio after transcription and keeps the notes and transcript instead.

Which is better for a sales organization?

Fireflies is the stronger choice because it includes CRM integrations, topics, soundbites, team analytics, and conversation intelligence. Granola fits an individual or team that wants lighter written notes.

Which supports more languages?

Fireflies publishes support for more than 100 languages, making it the safer choice for a highly multilingual organization.

Is Granola more private than Fireflies?

Granola stores less source media because it does not retain audio. Fireflies publishes a no-training commitment. Privacy still depends on transcript retention, sharing, workspace rules, and the consent process.
